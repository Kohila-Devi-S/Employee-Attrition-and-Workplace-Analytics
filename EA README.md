# 👥 Employee Attrition Analysis

## 📌 About
This project explores employee data to understand the patterns linked to employees leaving or staying at IBM.

## 📂 Dataset
[IBM HR Analytics Employee Attrition Dataset](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/pavansubhasht/ibm-hr-analytics-attrition-dataset)

## 🧰 Tools
Python, Pandas, NumPy, Matplotlib, Seaborn, and Jupyter Notebook.

## 🔍 Analysis
- Checked missing values and duplicate records.
- Compared attrition across departments and job roles.
- Studied overtime, monthly income, and job satisfaction.
- Explored distance from home, age, and work-life balance.
- Compared employees who left with those who stayed.

## 📊 Findings
- The overall employee attrition rate was **16.1%**.
- The **Sales department** had the highest attrition rate (**20.6%**).
- Employees who worked overtime had a higher attrition rate (**30.5%**) than those who did not (**10.4%**).
- Employees who left had a lower average monthly income (**4,787**) than employees who stayed (**6,833**).
- Attrition was highest among employees with the lowest job satisfaction rating (**22.8%**).
- **Sales Representatives** had the highest attrition rate among job roles (**39.8%**).
- Employees who left were younger on average (**33.6 years**) than those who stayed (**37.6 years**).
- Employees with the lowest work-life balance rating had the highest attrition rate (**31.3%**).
- Employees who left lived slightly farther from work on average (**10.6**) than those who stayed (**8.9**).

## 💡 Conclusion
The analysis found higher attrition among employees who worked overtime, had lower income or job satisfaction, and reported poor work-life balance. Attrition also differed by department, job role, age, and distance from home. The company can use these patterns to identify areas for further review and improve employee retention. These results show associations and do not prove that a factor directly causes employees to leave.
